III. Methodology

Data

We develop a benchmark of stylized multi-view images, since 3D reconstruction datasets like BlendedMVS and DTU provide ground-truth geometry but lack artistic variance.

As shown in the Figure below, we generate stylized variants (Impressionism, Oil painting, Engraving, Watercolour) of BlendedMVS while preserving 3D geometry. To do this we use TeleStyle, a content-preserving style-transfer model built on Qwen-Image-Edit-2509. It takes a content and a style reference image as input, producing a stylised output that preserves structure and composition while adopting the target aesthetic. To fit in the time and compute constraints, we stylize each frame in just four diffusion steps via a Lightning distillation for speed, keep only scenes with fewer than 150 views, and stylize every fifth image. We then obtain a benchmark of 80 scenes which contain 15 views on average. All with the ground truth camera positions and depth maps. The dataset can be found in this link.

Qualitative benchmark

Moreover, to assess model performance on our target use case, we collect images of three well-known architectural landmarks: Notre-Dame de Paris, the Rialto Bridge in Venice, and the Doge's Palace. Each are represented across three stylistic modalities: historical photographs, sketches, and paintings sourced from public archives. This selection is motivated by a concrete real-world scenario: recovering the 3D geometry of structures that have been transformed, damaged, or lost over time, for which only heterogeneous artistic documentation remains. This creates a mini benchmark for qualitative evaluation, as ground truth pointclouds are not available.

Evaluation

Before any architecture adaptation, we evaluate the baselines. We create an evaluation pipeline which evaluates the performance of MapAnything on our benchmark. To fit into memory, we feed a maximum of 8 views per scene. This does not hinder performance as our primary goal is style robustness and not scene coverage.

Destylization

A natural candidate for an additional baseline is to first remove the artistic style from each image before passing it to MapAnything—a destylization pre-processing step—so as to recover an approximation of the original photograph. Such a pipeline would test whether the reconstruction gap introduced by stylization (Table; 1) can be closed by style removal alone, without any architectural adaptation.

We explored two destylization strategies. The first, Reverse TeleStyle, reuses the TeleStyle pipeline in the reverse direction: the stylized image serves as the content input (Figure 1 in the model prompt) and the original photograph acts as the photorealistic style reference (Figure 2), instructing the model to transfer the photographic style back onto the content. The second strategy, ControlNet img2img, is motivated by the need to decouple style removal from scene content. A plain img2img diffusion pipeline risks hallucinating scene content, while conditioning on text alone provides no structural anchor. We therefore extract a depth map from the stylized image using a DPT estimator and use it as a geometric condition for a Stable Diffusion 1.5 ControlNet img2img pipeline: the stylized image initializes the diffusion process (preserving scene content) while the depth map constrains the geometry, and a photorealistic prompt — paired with a negative prompt penalising painterly and stylised textures — pushes the style toward realism. We swept the denoising strength across a wide range (0.10–0.70): at low strength the scene content is preserved but almost no style is removed, while higher strengths remove the style only by hallucinating away the geometry. No setting achieves both (see the slider below).

We quantify each destylized output by its DINOv2 cosine similarity to the original photograph, a proxy for semantic proximity. To probe the pipeline fully, we jointly swept its two coupled knobs — the img2img denoising strength and the ControlNet conditioning scale — and compared each combination against a matched baseline: the stylized inputs on the same frames (heatmap below; scene_0, eight frames across the four styles). Every combination falls below that stylized baseline of 0.85. The best cell (strength 0.20, conditioning scale 1.30) reaches only 0.78 — still 0.07 below the untouched stylized input, and it gets there only by barely altering the image. Pushing strength up to actually remove the style collapses similarity (to 0.19 at strength 0.65), and raising the conditioning scale recovers little.

Destylization therefore not only fails to recover photographic similarity but actively reduces it at every point on the grid: it cannot remove the artistic style without moving the image further from the photograph. As a pre-processing step it cannot close the reconstruction gap, so we discard it as a baseline and focus exclusively on architectural adaptations that operate directly on stylized inputs. (Our destylizer uses a Stable Diffusion 1.5 prior; a stronger generator could yield more photo-like outputs, but those would be hallucinated detail rather than recovered geometry, so the conclusion holds.)

Architecture Modifications

The MapAnything architecture uses the first 24 layers of a DINOv2-L model as its dense image encoder, with the subsequent 16 layers repurposed to initialize the Alternating Attention (AA) blocks. During training, the encoder was fine-tuned end-to-end with a conservative learning rate of 5×10−6 (versus 10−4 for the AA blocks and task heads), enabling it to specialize in depth-centric and view-consistent features.

We restrict all modifications exclusively to the DINOv2 encoder, keeping the downstream AA blocks and prediction heads frozen. Since DINOv2 was trained on natural photographs, stylized inputs induce severe out-of-distribution activations at this entry point; adapting the AA blocks would be counter-productive if the encoder fails to preserve the geometry-related signals required for multi-view matching.

Feature-Space Normalization

Instance Normalization is applied directly to intermediate feature tensors between DINOv2 layers, standardizing activations to 𝒩(0, 1) to strip style-specific texture statistics. This method is entirely training-free and serves as a lightweight baseline.

Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)

Learnable AdaIN affine layers are inserted between encoder layers and optimized via cross-domain distillation. A frozen teacher model processes photographs while a student (identical model with AdaIN layers) processes matched stylized inputs, supervised by an L1 loss on predicted depth maps (𝔻) and 3D point clouds (ℙ):

ℒ = α ‖ 𝔻student(Xstyle) − 𝔻teacher(Xphoto) ‖1  +  β ‖ ℙstudent(Xstyle) − ℙteacher(Xphoto) ‖1

By minimizing the discrepancy between the student’s predictions on stylized inputs and the teacher’s predictions on photographs, the encoder is encouraged to extract style-invariant geometric cues without altering downstream structural reasoning.

Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA)

Trainable low-rank adapter matrices are injected directly into the attention projections of MapAnything's DINOv2 backbone. Each targeted weight matrix W ∈ Rd×k is augmented with a factored residual update ΔW = BA, where B ∈ Rd×r, A ∈ Rr×k, and r ≪ min(d, k). During the forward pass the layer computes y = Wx + BAx: the frozen base projection and the low-rank correction are summed, so the adapter learns only the residual shift needed to handle stylized inputs. All base weights remain frozen during training; only the adapter parameters are updated.

The model is fine-tuned on BlendedMVS scenes rendered across multiple artistic styles (engraving, impressionism, oil painting, watercolour) using TeleStyle, with each training sample mixing styled and original views to encourage style-invariant predictions. An optional consistency regularization term additionally penalizes discrepancies between the model's 3D point predictions on styled versus original images:

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{consistency}} = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{i=1}^{V} \left\| P_i^{\text{styled}} - \text{sg}(P_i^{\text{original}}) \right\|_2^2\]

where sg(·) denotes the stop-gradient operator. This allows the adapter to learn style robustness directly from the domain shift observed at training time, while keeping the parameter overhead minimal.
