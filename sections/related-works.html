II. Related Works

Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction

Classical pipelines rely on structure-from-motion and multi-view stereo [1], [2], but recent models such as DUSt3R [3], MASt3R [4], MapAnything [5] and VGGT [6] regress geometry end-to-end from image collections. Alternative paradigms such as NeRF [7] and 3D Gaussian Splatting [8] achieve high fidelity but require per-scene optimization and dense photographic inputs, making them poorly suited to sparse or stylized historical sources. We build on MapAnything and address a setting none of these models were designed for: reconstruction under artistic style shift.

Robustness, Style, and Domain Shift

ImageNet-C [9] and RobustMVD [10] established that vision models degrade significantly under distribution shift. Stylized-ImageNet [11] further showed that learned features are often biased toward texture rather than shape — a direct concern for geometric reconstruction. We generate our stylized benchmark using diffusion-based stylization tools [12], [13], which enable consistent multi-view style transfer, critical for preserving cross-view coherence in our evaluation.

Encoders, Adaptation, and Heritage

MapAnything's frozen DINOv2 [14] encoder was not trained for style-invariant representations. We adapt it using LoRA [15], a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that minimizes computational overhead. Prior work on heritage reconstruction has mostly relied on photogrammetry [16]; work on painting-to-3D alignment [17] focused on viewpoint matching rather than dense reconstruction. Our work is the first to target dense geometry recovery from purely artistic inputs.
