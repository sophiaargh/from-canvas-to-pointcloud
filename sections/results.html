IV. Results

4.1 Robustness to Historical Image Degradation: Grayscale and Film

A key motivation of this project is reconstructing buildings from historical photographs, which constitute out-of-distribution (OOD) data relative to modern training sets. We compare the baseline model on standard RGB images against two degraded variants:

  • Grayscale — single-channel luminance representation replicated to three channels to satisfy the model's input requirements.
  • Simulated historical film — grayscale image with added Gaussian blur (radius = 1.0) and Gaussian noise (μ = 0, σ = 20) to mimic vintage lens softness and silver-halide film grain.

Contrary to our initial hypothesis, AbsRel remains statistically indistinguishable across all three variants for every artistic style, as evidenced by the heavily overlapping error bars. This reveals that MapAnything's DINOv2-based encoder relies primarily on geometric and structural cues rather than chromatic or low-level textural information, making it inherently robust to color loss, lens blur, and film grain — and therefore viable for direct deployment on historical archive imagery without domain adaptation.

4.2 Feature-Space Normalization Experiments

Our hypothesis was that stylized inputs induce covariate shift in DINOv2's intermediate activations, and that training-free Instance Normalization (IN) toward 𝒩(0, 1) would act as a style-invariant regularizer. We ran an ablation over two axes:

  • Number of normalized layers — from 1 to 24.
  • Cascading direction — front normalization (layers 0, 1, 2, …) vs. end normalization (layers 23, 22, 21, …), evaluated on 20 stylized scenes.

Normalization uniformly degrades performance across all styles, with AbsRel increasing monotonically with the number of normalized layers in both directions. The two strategies exhibit a notable crossover behaviour: end normalization achieves lower AbsRel at small layer counts (1–4), but its performance deteriorates steeply and overtakes front normalization around layers 8–12, after which it becomes the more harmful of the two.

This suggests that normalizing a small number of late-stage layers — where representations are more abstract and style-specific variance is lower — introduces only marginal disruption. However, as more late layers are normalized, the cumulative destruction of the high-level semantic structure feeding directly into the Alternating Attention (AA) block becomes severe. Front normalization, by contrast, degrades early spatial cues immediately but does so more uniformly, resulting in a slower degradation curve. In both cases, the core conclusion holds: training-free instance normalization cannot disentangle style statistics from geometric signal, directly motivating our learnable AdaIN approach.

4.3 Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)

To overcome the destructive effect of raw normalization, we evaluated trainable AdaIN layers optimized via cross-domain distillation: a frozen teacher model processes photographs while a student model (identical architecture with AdaIN layers inserted) processes matched stylized inputs, supervised by an L1 loss on predicted depth maps and 3D point clouds:

ℒ  =  α   &lVert; 𝒟student(Xstyle) − 𝒟teacher(Xphoto) &rVert;1  +  β   &lVert; 𝒫student(Xstyle) − 𝒫teacher(Xphoto) &rVert;1

where 𝒟 and 𝒫 denote predicted depth maps and point clouds respectively, and α, β are scaling hyperparameters. All MapAnything weights remain frozen; only the AdaIN affine parameters are updated over one epoch on 800 paired image sets.

We ran the same ablation as above — varying the number of AdaIN layers (1–24) and cascading direction.

The outcome mirrors the IN experiments: AdaIN uniformly underperforms the unmodified baseline, end cascading is consistently less harmful than front at low layer counts before both converge, and more layers correlates with steeper degradation. This suggests the failure is not merely uncontrolled variance scaling but reflects a more fundamental limitation: lightweight affine transformations cannot bridge the complex domain gap between artistic and photographic inputs. While AdaIN shifts global style statistics, it likely disrupts the fine-grained spatial correlations within DINOv2's attention maps that are critical for multi-view geometric reasoning. This result directly justifies the move to LoRA, which operates within the attention projections themselves rather than on feature statistics.
